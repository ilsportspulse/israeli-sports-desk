# Israel Sports Pulse × Maccabi World Union — eight-week pilot outline

Status date: 16 July 2026  
Visibility: internal proposal draft. No contact has been made, no relationship is implied and nothing may be sent without owner approval.

## Proposed route

**Prospective function:** Director of Development  
**Current role verification:** https://www.maccabi.org/who-we-are/staff  
**Official contact route:** https://www.maccabi.org/contact  
**Proposed term:** eight consecutive weeks after a written pilot start date.  
**Opening ask:** distribution, archival cooperation and two relevant territorial introductions — not editorial influence and not an unrestricted donation request.

Maccabi World Union describes itself as the umbrella body for six confederations and 70 territorial organisations. That international structure closely matches ILSP's aim of making Israeli sport more accessible to English-reading audiences in Israel and abroad.

## Pilot objective

Test whether a concise English Israeli-sport product can create measurable, repeat engagement among selected Maccabi communities while giving ILSP disciplined evidence for product, language and distribution decisions.

The pilot is deliberately small. It does not announce a permanent partnership, promise exclusive access or give either party rights beyond the agreed eight-week work.

## Proposed audience

One or two English-speaking Maccabi territorial organisations selected jointly after interest is confirmed. The initial audience should be narrow enough to measure and support properly; it should not attempt to cover all 70 territories at once.

## ILSP deliverables

1. **Eight weekly English Israeli-sport briefings** — one mobile-first edition per week linking to independently produced ILSP reporting across leagues, athletes abroad, women, youth and Olympic sport where the news merits inclusion.
2. **Two original archive features** — researched from claim-specific federation, competition, club, museum or Maccabi records, with contemporary evidence distinguished from later recollection.
3. **Four five-question quiz activations** — source-backed and suitable for general audiences, with no betting prompts or unverifiable trivia.
4. **One clearly labelled pilot landing page** — presenting the weekly editions and explaining the limited collaboration without implying institutional control over ILSP coverage.
5. **One end-of-pilot report** — delivery record, available engagement metrics, lessons, unresolved rights issues and a recommendation to stop, repeat or redesign.

## Requested Maccabi World Union contribution

- Nominate one development or programme coordinator for the pilot.
- Introduce ILSP to two suitable territorial organisations or community programme leads.
- Where desired, distribute the weekly link through agreed opt-in or existing community channels.
- Identify primary or authoritative archive records relevant to the two historical features.
- Provide written permission for any supplied logo, archive item, quotation or programme description before ILSP uses it.

No cash contribution is required for this first pilot. Any later funding, service cost, travel, event access or broader licence would require a separate proposal and owner approval.

## Measurement framework

No audience figure is promised in advance. Subject to privacy-safe analytics being operational, the pilot can report:

- briefings delivered on time;
- participating territories and distribution actions;
- unique landing-page visits and returning visitors;
- briefing click-through rate;
- quiz starts and completion rate;
- archive-feature reading depth;
- voluntary newsletter sign-ups with valid consent;
- qualitative feedback collected by a short, optional survey.

ILSP will not share subscriber-level data. Reporting should be aggregated, proportionate and compliant with the final privacy policy.

## Editorial and commercial firewall

- ILSP alone chooses stories, headlines, rankings, conclusions, corrections and publication timing.
- Maccabi World Union may correct factual descriptions of its own programmes and flag rights concerns, but it receives no pre-publication approval of independent reporting.
- The pilot label must clearly describe the collaboration; it may not imply endorsement of every ILSP article.
- Critical or unfavourable coverage cannot be suppressed because of the pilot.
- No exclusivity, category lock-out, right of first refusal or automatic renewal is included.
- Any sponsored or supplied material is labelled and recorded in the internal rights ledger.

## Rights and safeguarding

- Every archive photograph, document, video and logo requires item-specific permission or a verifiable reusable licence.
- Permission must state the allowed platforms, term, geography, cropping, credit wording and archive-retention rights.
- ILSP will not republish a photograph merely because it appears on a website or social account.
- Youth coverage uses no betting promotion and avoids unnecessary personal data.
- Neither party uploads a private mailing list into the other's systems during the pilot.

## Proposed timetable

| Stage | Timing | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Approval and scoping | Before week 1 | Named coordinators, two territories, rights rules and measurement plan |
| Pilot preparation | Week 0 | Landing-page draft, briefing template and first content calendar |
| Live delivery | Weeks 1–8 | Eight briefings, two archive features and four quizzes |
| Midpoint review | End of week 4 | Delivery and engagement check; safe corrections only |
| Final review | End of week 8 | Aggregated report and stop/repeat/redesign recommendation |

## Success gate

The pilot succeeds if all agreed editions are delivered, no material rights or editorial-independence breach occurs, the selected territories actually distribute the product, and the final data is sufficient to make a reasoned next-step decision. A large audience is not required for the experiment to be useful.
